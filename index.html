Act 1 · The setup

This is GFP. It glows.

Green fluorescent protein comes from the jellyfish Aequorea victoria and made modern cell biology visible. In 2016, Sarkisyan and colleagues mutated it at random and measured how brightly 51,715 versions glowed.

That's a rare thing: a real map of what works and what doesn't for an entire protein. 58% of the measured versions still glow visibly.

The rules. We're exploring a known map, and the strategies don't get to see it. They can only propose versions that were actually measured, so every guess gets a real score. A real lab isn't limited to a precomputed library, and that's the price of honest scoring.

Act 3 · The lesson

What the race showed

So what?

Drug and enzyme design face the same landscape at far larger scale. Every lab measurement costs days and money, so whatever proposes the next batch of variants decides how fast a project moves. The best tools today combine evolutionary information, structure and learned models. They're judged the same way this page judges its strategies: against real measurements.

What this page doesn't do

  • Strategies can only pick variants that were measured; a real campaign can make any mutation.
  • The model scores add up single-mutation effects and ignore epistasis on purpose.
  • The models are small: ESM-2 35M, a site-independent profile, and a 3B-parameter LLM.
  • The experiment's protein carries F64L relative to UniProt P42212; the 3D model is the AlphaFold model of P42212.
  • It isn't a design tool or a benchmark (see ProteinGym). Don't take anything here to a lab bench.
